The regenerated dataset uses seed 20261028.
- **Timing.** Single-worker ASWIFT median latency was 41.85 ms on the M5 Pro and 270.52 ms on the Ryzen 5 2400G. The desktop median alone exceeds 200 ms. ## Implementation and SI Table S3

- **Smoothing.** The manuscript fitter uses a second-difference pentadiagonal positive-definite system and a banded solve. A solve at fixed bandwidth scales linearly in trace length. Total fitting time also depends on the number of λ candidates and solver iterations. The pilot uses the unweighted L-curve. Curvature uses numerical gradients of the log residual and log roughness coordinates with respect to log λ. The weighted λ search is followed by a Huber IRLS fit with tolerance and an iteration cap (25 in the manuscript implementation). Describe one weighted λ reselection, rather than a single weight update.
- **Search grids.** The default unweighted L-curve uses 200 points from 10⁻⁵ to 10⁸. The Huber-weighted search uses 200 from 10⁻¹ to 10⁸. Derpsalsa uses 50 candidates from 10⁻¹ to 10⁷ followed by a ten-point local refinement. It minimizes the sum of fitted baseline samples inside the full-prominence window among candidates with sufficient weight on both sides. For the reported peak-area metric, the installed package interpolates the full-prominence endpoints and integrates the baseline-subtracted trace by the trapezoidal rule.
- **Fixed settings.** Huber cutoff 2, rolling MAD window 21, and full-prominence baseline selection agree with the implementation. S15 found the smallest absolute pooled bias at relative height 0.3. The benchmark/package metadata record a peak-region default of **0.5**. Report the sweep result and the setting actually used separately. Changing the manuscript value alone cannot establish that 0.3 was used in a specific fit.
- **Derpsalsa.** The installed pybaselines 1.2.1 implementation confirms defaults of p = 0.01, k = SD(input)/10, difference order 2, 50 iterations, tolerance 0.001, smoothing half-window floor(N/200) and 16 smoothing passes. ASWIFT supplies its smoothed trace as input and selects λ itself. Suggested wording: “We used the derpsalsa defaults and evaluated their sensitivity on the simulated benchmark.”
- **Peak selection and software versions.** The manuscript implementation selects the most prominent peak. The separate local ASWIFT working tree (base commit c657d2d, with uncommitted edits to the viewer, workflow and fitter) exposes an optional expected-potential interval in batch fitting and a corresponding viewer control, plus current inversion in the viewer workflow. The installed 1.0.3 distribution used in the timing run does not expose those named controls. Specify the release or source revision when describing the newer features. A separate installed desktop executable and an inversion argument on the core package fit API have not been verified.
- **Comparators and scope.** In peak_extraction/extract_peaks.py, polynomial fitting tests degrees 2 through the configured maximum (default 4), using weighted residual loss and a degree penalty (default 0). Multi-Gaussian initializes the signal center from the most prominent peak and the baseline from the lowest current decile, and sets explicit amplitude, center and width bounds. EWD defaults to the fk8 wavelet and eight extension cycles. The revision adapter uses the same method functions across datasets. Experimental preprocessing differs, including joint-channel masking in vivo. Historical method-output CSVs do not independently establish every configuration used to generate them. Avoid claiming that only ASWIFT ran without parameter changes or that every historical configuration was identical.

## Simulation results

- **Figures 2 and S2.** Figure 2 has 10,800 traces (4 families × 30 shapes × 10 SNRs × 3 widths × 3 locations), a 1 µA peak and 400 points from −0.4 to 0 V. SNR spans 15–60 dB in 5 dB steps. In the rebuilt Figure 2 summary, pooled ASWIFT bias was −0.0042 µA and RMS within-family SD was 0.0254 µA. The corresponding S2 values were −0.0077 and 0.0263 µA. ASWIFT had the smallest absolute across-family mean bias and lowest RMS within-family SD at every SNR in both summaries. Use “lower variability” or “substantially lower variability” for these descriptive comparisons.
- **Baseline and noise generation.** The generator constructs linear, convex quadratic, exponential and external-center two-Gaussian backgrounds. It restricts the generating shapes but contains no explicit second-peak detection and rejection pass. The shape generators have fixed seeds. The saved Figure 2 realization is available; a global seed for its historical additive noise was not established. The S2 generator samples 0–20% of points per trace and adds Laplace noise with scale 0.12. The illustrative upper panels use 15%.
- **Figure 3.** ASWIFT had the lowest normalized SD at all 26 plotted conditions and the reference. Across all 27 amplitudes its mean absolute deviation of the mean raw height from truth was 0.0048 µA. After normalization, mean-curve errors were similar across methods (0.0044–0.0055 in normalized units); multi-Gaussian had the smallest of these errors. The figure contains no calculated LOD. Suggested wording: “The smaller standard deviations suggest a potential improvement in detection limit.”
- **S3.** A 30 dB noise realization is paired across tail heights and methods for each of 100 replicates. ASWIFT mean biases at 1×, 2×, 4×, 8× and 16× were −0.008, −0.017, −0.043, −0.174 and −0.478 µA, respectively, for a true 1 µA peak. All 2,000 fits were finite.
- **S4.** Mean ASWIFT bias was +0.2175 µA overall, +0.2146 for exponential, +0.3107 for polynomial, +0.3363 for multi-Gaussian and +0.0084 for linear backgrounds (2,700 traces per family). Only 79.3% of multi-Gaussian traces were concave throughout the true peak window. RMS within-family SD across family/SNR groups was 0.121 µA for ASWIFT, 0.166 for polynomial, 0.167 for EWD and 0.227 for multi-Gaussian.
- **S13–S15.** For Huber settings 0, 1, 2 and 3, pooled mean biases were −0.0402, −0.0127, −0.0077 and −0.0064 µA. Cutoff 2 substantially improves on disabling reweighting, but cutoff 3 has slightly smaller absolute bias. Window sizes 11–51 gave RMS SDs of 0.0222–0.0234 µA. For relative heights 0.1, 0.3, 0.5 and 1.0, mean biases were +0.0143, −0.0057, −0.0083 and −0.0208 µA.
- **S16–S19.** results/derpsalsa/selection_table.csv gives errors and failures for all 12 settings. Reference mean bias and RMSE were −0.00330 and 0.02685 µA. The only failure was one solver error among 10,800 attempts at p = 0.001. Halving/doubling k gave RMSE 0.02795/0.02699 µA. Orders 1 and 3 gave RMSE 0.06076 and 0.03767 µA with negative and positive bias, respectively. More iterations had negligible effect. Smoothing variants gave RMSE 0.02654–0.02696 µA.

- **Rejection and minimum ratio.** The rejection rule compares peak height with twice the **mean absolute residual** of the robust smooth. No-peak detection is a separate failure condition. The value 3.37294 is the minimum for the current full-doxorubicin refit, at zero-based source row 2404 (channel 4, 10 Hz, file 10hz-41.csv). With the historical saved height used for Figure 4, that trace gives 3.28457 against the reconstructed mean absolute residual. Keep the numerators explicit. The minimum height/median absolute residual across the audited figure inputs was 6.28046 (blood, source row 62). The audit includes all five doxorubicin frequencies for full refits, both 10-MAD-masked in vivo channels, and all 180 ATP channel-0 scans including buffer and washout. Figure 4 membership is narrower. No ASWIFT rejections were recorded in these audits.
- **Figure 4 and S5.** Figure 4 enables a quantitative filter on normalized mean points: SD must be at most one-half the mean. S5 disables it and shows the 10 Hz row. The plotting function uses the first frequency in its input list. This is a display rule for concentration summaries. At 20 µM and 10 Hz the normalized means ± sample SD were 0.543 ± 0.026 for ASWIFT, 0.786 ± 0.288 for polynomial, 3.839 ± 2.008 for multi-Gaussian and 0.089 ± 0.136 for EWD. “Stable” should be tied to dispersion and consistency with the measured traces.
- **Figure 5, S12 and Table S4.** The joint-channel mask uses a centered 21-point rolling median and a channel-wide scaled MAD of residuals. In ISF it ranges from 0.0134 to 0.0137; polynomial has lower scatter at 10 and 20 MAD (about 0.0087). Multi-Gaussian returned finite blood estimates for all 206 samples in each condition. ## All-dataset baseline checks

The full-scope audit in results/experimental_checks covers 3,592 successful trace fits: all 3,000 doxorubicin traces at 10, 100, 200, 500 and 700 Hz; all 412 blood/ISF traces at 200 Hz with joint-channel 10-MAD masking; and all 180 ATP channel-0 traces at 150, 200 and 250 Hz, including buffer, washout and rechallenge scans.

- **Tail claim.** Subtract the fitted baseline beneath the peak from the larger of the two smoothed endpoint currents and divide by the fitted peak height. Exactly 136 traces exceeded 4×, all at 10 Hz in the doxorubicin dataset. The maximum was 10.90×. Replacing the endpoint current with the maximum smoothed current anywhere outside the peak window gives the same 136 exceedances. This measures tail rise in the smoothed experimental signal.
- **Endpoint-line claim.** Within each selected peak window, compare the fitted baseline with the straight line joining its endpoint values. No fitted baseline rose above that line by more than 1% of the fitted peak height. The largest upward departure was 0.168%, in the kanamycin data. The baseline selection favors low-area fits in the peak window. This describes the geometry of the selected fits and cannot establish whether the underlying experimental backgrounds were concave.

Reproduce both checks with the full-scope curvature command in the publication inventory. The per-trace CSV preserves the dataset, frequency, channel and source row for each result.

## Smoothed-flank second differences

The analysis in results/smoothed_flank_curvature recomputes the manuscript robust smoother for all 3,592 experimental traces and uses the saved peak windows from experimental_checks. The primary span extends half a peak-window width outward on each side, using only observed points strictly outside the window. Each second-difference triple lies entirely inside one flank. The trace-level mean pools both flanks' triples equally. At least five points per flank are required.

All 3,592 traces had positive mean second differences. At the primary width, each flank separately also had a nonnegative mean in every trace. The pooled sign remained positive with spans of one-quarter and one full window width. At the quarter-width setting, five 10 Hz doxorubicin traces had a negative mean on one flank, with a positive pooled mean. Accounting for unequal voltage spacing after masking using second divided differences also gave positive pooled means for all traces at all three spans.

Suggested wording: “As an additional check using the smoothed measured signal, we calculated the mean second difference on the flanks immediately outside each selected peak window. With each flank extending half the peak-window width, both flanks had nonnegative mean second differences in all 3,592 experimental traces. The pooled mean remained nonnegative when the flank span was varied from one-quarter to one full window width.”

This supports convexity on average over the observed flanks. It does not establish pointwise convexity or the curvature of the unobserved background beneath the peak. Peak tails contribute to the smoothed signal, and the window location remains ASWIFT-derived. The statistic uses no fitted-baseline values.
